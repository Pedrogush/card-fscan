# Shingle Scanner v2: fabrication pack

Camera stand + tilted-slot card racks. All STLs are in print orientation (flat side on the bed).
Generated by `shingle_cad.py` / `export_parts.py` (Python, numpy only). Change a parameter there and re-run
to regenerate every part. Units: mm. NOT yet test-printed: print the groove test first.

## Print order
1. `groove_test_left` + `groove_test_right` (about 30 g, under 1 h). Hold them 64 mm apart, grooves facing
   each other, and slide real unsleeved cards in. The grooves are 0.8 / 0.9 / 1.0 / 1.1 / 1.2 mm wide
   (narrowest at the cut corner). Pick the width where a card slides down on its own but doesn't rattle.
   The racks use 1.0 mm; tell whoever maintains the script if another width wins.
2. One rack. Load 20 cards, take a photo, run the reader. Only then print the rest.
3. The stand.

## Parts per rack (1 rack = 1 column of 20 cards)
| File | Qty | Size (mm) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| rack_wall_left.stl | 1 | 302 x 33 x 4.5 | Grooved face up. Needs a 256 x 256 bed, placed diagonally |
| rack_wall_right.stl | 1 | 302 x 33 x 4.5 | Mirror of the left wall |
| rack_floor.stl | 1 | 199 x 64 x 3 | Or cut from 3 mm MDF/hardboard (any hardware store or carpenter) |
| rack_header.stl | 1 | 64 x 24.5 x 3 | Or 3 mm MDF. The marker sticker goes on top (see below) |

Assembly: glue the floor between the walls at the back (under the grooves), and the header on the front
ledges, top flush with the wall tops. CA glue or 2-part epoxy. Racks must be exactly 70 mm wide.

How many: C4 needs 4 racks per photo, C6 needs 6. Start with one set; a second set lets volunteers fill
while the camera shoots.

## Stand (one per station)
| File | Qty | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| stand_foot.stl | 1 | Screws to the baseboard; front edge = rack stop |
| stand_post_1/2/3.stl | 1 each | Pegged joints. Holes every 10 mm; notches mark lens heights 250/300/350/400 |
| stand_carriage.stl | 1 | Plate-side down (195 x 246 mm, fits a 256 bed). Arm reaches 160 mm; 70 x 70 mm lens window |
| stand_phone_fence.stl | 1 | Straight edge for the phone; 2 x M3 x 16 bolts + nuts through the plate slots |

Hardware: 600 x 450 mm plywood/MDF baseboard, 4 wood screws (M4/M5), one 5 mm steel pin or M5 x 50 bolt,
rubber bands for the phone.

Heights here are measured from the TOP of the racks (33 mm above the board), not from paper strips.
Hole for lens height h sits at h + 8 mm above the board.

## Settings and material (estimates)
PLA or PETG, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, 4 walls, 25% infill (rack walls end up nearly solid).
- One rack, walls only: about 110 g, 5-7 h. Floor and header printed too: +55 g.
- Stand: about 350-450 g, 15-20 h.
- Stand + 6 racks (walls only) + test coupons: roughly one 1 kg spool.

## Marker stickers (spec conformance)
`header_stickers.svg` is a 1:1 sheet with the six header stickers (columns 1-6, ArUco DICT_4X4_50
ids 0-11). Open it in a browser and print at 100% / Actual size; check the 100 mm ruler. Each sticker is
exactly the header plate (64 x 24.5 mm): cut on the outline and stick it flush with the plate edges, the
"card 1 edge" towards the cards. That puts the markers at strip x 6-24 / 46-64 mm and y 3.5-21.5 mm, as
`spec/SPEC.md` section 1 requires. Use matte paper; glossy labels glare. Stop cards are unchanged (print
pack page 5); a stop card goes in the next groove after the last card.

## Phone plate
The phone lies screen up in **landscape**: its long side runs across the columns, because the frame's long
side must (C4 360 x 270, C6 480 x 360 mm). The main lens goes over the window centre (crosshair notches)
and the phone body extends towards column 1 (+x), which is why the plate is 195 x 110 mm and off-centre
rather than a square. The fence registers the phone's long edge so the phone goes back to the same place
every time. Set it once per phone: loosen the two M3 bolts, slide it until the lens is on the crosshair
(lens-to-edge distance 10-28 mm covers common phones), tighten, and confirm with the calibration photo.
Phones with a camera island larger than 70 x 70 mm (e.g. full-width camera bars) rest on the island; add
foam pads at the far end to keep them level.

## Geometry notes
- Rack front = strip origin (spec y = 0). Card 1's top edge is 25 mm behind it, the header covers 0-24.5.
- All name-bar top edges and the marker stickers lie in one plane, H = 33.4 mm above the board. Lens
  heights in the height table are measured from this plane.
- Cards tilt 20 degrees. Seen from above, each card still shows exactly 10 mm, so the spec's slot grid is
  unchanged, but each name bar slopes down by about 3.6 mm from its top edge. At C6 heights that shifts
  text by at most about 2.5 mm at the frame corners (parallax); the reader finds the text line in each slot,
  so this is absorbed.
- Why 20 degrees: the ribs between grooves are 10 x sin(tilt) - 1.0 mm thick. At 20 degrees that is 2.4 mm
  (printable). 15 degrees gives 1.6 mm ribs (fragile), 30 degrees makes the rack 47 mm tall and the bars drop
  5.8 mm. Cards don't have to slide on their own: push each one down until it rests on the floor; the floor,
  not gravity, sets the height.
- Lens window centre is 160 mm from the post = strip y 120, the middle of the frame.

## Viewing the files
- STLs: F3D (fast viewer; drag files in, or `f3d rack_wall_left.stl`) or PrusaSlicer (also slices them).
- Measuring: FreeCAD (open the STL, Measure tool).
- Preview renders: `cd source && uv run --with numpy --with opencv-python-headless --with pillow python scenes.py`
  writes `renders/*.png`. Regenerate STLs with `uv run --with numpy python export_parts.py`, stickers with
  `uv run --with numpy --with opencv-python-headless python header_stickers.py`.
